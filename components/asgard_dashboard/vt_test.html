The virtual thermostat is the thermostat of this zone in every scenario. Scenario — choose the one that matches your wiring
All four scenarios run the zone on the virtual thermostat; they only differ in which temperature sensor it heats and cools against. That choice, together with the dip switches and the master of the zone, decides the expected values on the next screens. The default is A; change it here at any time.
A · Virtual Thermostat + external sensor SW1-8 OFFdefault
The virtual thermostat reads its own external sensor: a DS18x20 on the board, or a temperature pushed over the REST API. No Mitsubishi wireless thermostat or CNRF is fitted, so the heat pump uses the sensor of its own controller. MRC Sensor setting: MRC.
B1 · Virtual Thermostat + Mitsubishi wireless OR CNRF SW1-8 ON
The virtual thermostat takes the room temperature from the Mitsubishi wireless thermostat (RCx) or CNRF over the bus: the wireless remote in the room stays the only sensor, and it is also the master of the zone. MRC Sensor setting: Room RCx.
B2 · Virtual Thermostat + Mitsubishi wireless OR CNRF, external sensor SW1-8 ON
A Mitsubishi wireless thermostat or CNRF is fitted and keeps measuring for the heat pump, but the virtual thermostat does not use it: an external sensor of your own (DS18x20 or REST API) is the room temperature Odin heats against. MRC Sensor setting: MRC.
D · Virtual Thermostat + MRC SW1-8 OFF
The virtual thermostat reads the room temperature from the MRC itself, the sensor built into the wired controller, so no extra sensor is needed. MRC Sensor setting: MRC.
If you are not sure: SW1-8 ON means a Mitsubishi wireless thermostat or CNRF module is in use — that is scenario B1 or B2, otherwise A or D.

Thermostat is currently not OFF.
With the thermostat of this zone on OFF nothing else drives the relay, so you can switch the heating by hand to test the wiring. Switching it to OFF also clears the ODIN relay override of this zone, so there is nothing to wait for: the board is ready as soon as it reports the zone OFF. The next step brings this zone's flow setpoint back to 27 °C when a DHW cycle left it higher, because above that number the heating does not answer to a contact closed by hand. The test also refuses while the heat pump is running a DHW or a legionella cycle.
